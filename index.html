Lipa Namba

Same-network cost. One number to remember.

Any Lipa Namba can already receive money from any network in Tanzania today. That was never the problem. The real cost is that a cross-network payment costs more than a same-network one, which is why a shop ends up with four or five Lipa Namba stickers at the counter, one per network, just to keep every customer's payment cheap.

Pointpesa replaces all of those stickers with a single number. A customer scans or dials your Pointpesa ID, picks which network they're paying from inside WhatsApp, and gets automatically routed to your matching Lipa Namba for that network, whether that's M-Pesa, Airtel Money, Mixx by Yas, HaloPesa, or a bank account. They still get the same-network rate. You still keep every number you had before. You just stop needing to remember or display more than one.

Serving five networks shouldn't mean five different numbers.

This isn't only a shop problem. Retailers, service businesses, and mobile money agents who handle cash-in and cash-out all day run into the same friction, often several times an hour.

A different Lipa Namba for every network, just to keep costs down

It's not that one Lipa Namba can't receive a payment from another network, it already can. It's that doing so costs more. So merchants hand out whichever sticker matches the customer's network, one for M-Pesa, one for Airtel, one for a bank QR, one for Mixx, just to avoid the higher cross-network fee.

No single record of who paid what

Sales, cash-ins, and cash-outs land in four or five separate apps. Closing out the day's books means checking that many phones and screenshots just to add it all up.

Support lives on hold, not on WhatsApp

When a payment fails, whether it's a sale, a cash-in, or a cash-out, you're stuck calling a hotline instead of getting an answer where you already spend your day.
